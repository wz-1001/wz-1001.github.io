My Background

My path into tech isn't a standard one, and I think that's a strength. I studied BEng Chemical Engineering, which taught me how to look at highly complex, dynamic systems, find the bottlenecks, and design for efficiency.

I shifted into Data Science to build the intelligent tools that optimise those bottlenecks. I'm currently finishing my MSc at the University of Liverpool, where my research focuses heavily on "Green AI." I like asking practical questions: do we really need a massive, compute-heavy deep learning model for a problem, or will a simpler, more energy-efficient algorithm do the job just as well?
